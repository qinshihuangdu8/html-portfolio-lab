Woven on a Backstrap Loom

Dhaka fabric gets its geometric pattern from a supplementary weft technique — extra threads worked in by hand as the weaver goes, row by row, rather than printed on afterward.

Most weavers in the hills still use a simple backstrap loom, tied at one end to a post or tree and at the other around the weaver's waist. It's slow work: a single meter of cloth can take the better part of a day.

Pashmina, by contrast, comes from the soft undercoat of mountain goats reared at high altitude, spun into a fine, warm yarn that's been traded out of Nepal and the Himalayas for centuries.
